Rama Krishna: 5th Generation Kondapalli Artisan

Rama Krishna was born in the toy-making village of Kondapalli, where the scent of Tella Poniki wood and natural dyes fills the air. He learned the craft by watching his grandfather and father shape blocks of wood into intricate mythological figures, animals, and scenes of rural life.

The Process of Creation

The creation of a Kondapalli toy is an incredibly labor-intensive process that Rama Krishna has perfected over his 35-year career:

  • Sourcing the Wood: The journey begins in the nearby hills, where artisans carefully harvest Tella Poniki, a locally available softwood that is light and easily malleable. The wood is naturally seasoned in the sun for weeks.
  • Rough Carving: Using specialized knives and chisels, Rama Krishna roughly shapes the wood into the basic form of the toy, relying solely on his memory and traditional proportions.
  • Fine Detailing & Assembly: The intricate details are carved out. For complex figures, different parts are carved separately and joined together. A paste made of tamarind seed powder and sawdust, known as Makku, is applied to fill joints and smooth out the edges, ensuring a seamless finish.
  • Painting: Once the Makku dries and the surface is sanded smooth, the true magic happens. Rama Krishna's wife and daughters typically take over the painting process, using brilliant, water-based natural colors—ochre, vegetable dyes, and soot—to give the toys their signature vibrant appearance.

"When I carve a toy, I am not just shaping wood; I am breathing life into a piece of our history," says Rama Krishna. Today, purchasing an authentic Kondapalli toy from Rama Krishna isn't just about bringing home a decorative item. It is about supporting a centuries-old tradition and empowering his family to keep this beautiful art form alive for the next generation.
